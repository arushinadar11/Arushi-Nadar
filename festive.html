Overview

A national festive campaign, localised for each region of India.

Between August and October, India celebrates a succession of regional festivals. During my internship at Landor India, I contributed to Coca-Cola’s festive season campaign: a suite of illustrated key visuals adapted into posters, banners and in-store displays for retail outlets across the country, each in the language of its region.

ClientCoca-Cola India
StudioLandor India (internship)
DeliverablesKey visuals, posters, banners, outdoor and in-store displays
Year2024

concept

The brief

One campaign across many regional celebrations.

India’s festive season encompasses many distinct celebrations, including Onam in Kerala, Navratri and Durga Puja, Dussehra in the north and Dasara in Karnataka, each with its own music, dress, rituals and language.

The brief was to create a campaign that remained unmistakably Coca-Cola nationwide, while feeling authentic and relevant to audiences in each region.

The idea

Coca-Cola at the heart of the celebration.

Each visual features a character captured mid-celebration, playing dandiya, beating a chenda drum or sounding a horn, while enjoying a Coca-Cola. Every character is dressed for their regional festival and rendered in a vibrant, folk-inspired illustration style.

The contour bottle is woven subtly into the patterns of clothing and instruments. The headline follows a consistent message across languages: open a Coca-Cola and bring the festival to life.

Dussehra poster in Hindi: “Coca-Cola khulega, Dussehra mein dhoom machega”.

progress

Key visuals

A consistent system with regional expression.

Core brand elements remained fixed across every execution: the red background, the Spencerian script logo, the product bottle and the yellow price badge, with the headline set in bold yellow type in the local script.

Regional character came through illustration. Each market received its own figures and cultural references, from dandiya sticks for Navratri to a caparisoned elephant and parasol for Mysuru Dasara, allowing one framework to feel distinct in every state.

Navratri banner in Gujarati; Dasara gate arch in Kannada; Dussehra banner in Hindi; Onam bus shelter in Malayalam.

In-store

Adapting the campaign for retail.

As the campaign’s primary touchpoint was the point of sale, a significant part of the work involved adapting the key visuals into retail formats: counter-top units, floor-standing displays, entrance arches and fridge wraps.

Each format had specific dimensions and die-cut shapes, requiring the illustrations to be re-composed for every application while maintaining headline legibility at a distance and accommodating product placement.

Navratri counter-top display and floor stand; Navratri entrance arch; Dasara floor-standing shelf and counter-top display in Kannada.

outcome

Result

Visible from street to shelf.

The campaign ran throughout the festive season across posters, banners, outdoor sites and retail outlets nationwide, with executions in Hindi, Gujarati, Marathi, Kannada and Malayalam alongside English.

The project gave me first-hand experience of localising a global brand at scale: maintaining one consistent idea while tailoring it for every region, format and festival.

The Navratri retail range, ready for stores.
