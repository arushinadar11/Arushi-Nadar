Overview

A wellbeing app helping mothers build small moments of calm into their day.

Liv is a collaborative project developed in a team of four designers. We set out to design an emotional wellbeing app for mothers with limited time for self-care, offering short, supportive interactions rather than demanding routines. I led the brand identity, designing the logo and colour palette, and contributed to applying that identity across the app interface.

concept

The problem

Self-care for those with the least time for it.

Many mothers aged 25 to 50 balance work, parenting and household responsibilities with little time for themselves. This sustained mental load often leads to emotional fatigue and anxiety.

Existing wellness apps are frequently too time-consuming, generic or overwhelming for this audience. Our persona, Emma, a working mother managing meetings and childcare from the same kitchen table, kept these constraints central to our decisions.

The idea

Micro-moments of wellbeing, inspired by hygge.

The concept draws on the Danish principle of hygge: comfort, connection and contentment. Liv is positioned as a calm companion rather than a coach, offering brief activities that fit realistically into a busy day.

Key features include mood tracking and daily affirmations, time-flexible meditation, mood-based music, gratitude journaling, and gentle reminders to hydrate, stretch or pause for a breath.

My part · Brand identity

An identity built on warmth and calm.

The logo distils the app’s purpose into simple forms. It combines four soft curves representing a hug, a person, a smile and a moment, which together form an approachable face alongside the “liv” wordmark.

The palette deliberately avoids clinical or high-energy colours. Warm creams and sand tones are paired with a soft sky blue and a muted grey to create a calm, reassuring atmosphere throughout the experience.

The logo, built from four ideas (hug, human, smile and moment), and applied to a tote bag.

Interface

From wireframes to a considered interface.

The team began with wireframes to define the user flow, prioritising how quickly a user could check in, begin a meditation or add a journal entry with minimal effort.

I contributed to translating the brand into the interface, applying the palette and soft, rounded forms to keep each screen light and uncluttered. Navigation was kept simple and icon-based with clear visual cues, designed for users who are often fatigued and operating one-handed.

Top: early wireframes. Bottom: designed screens for meditation, music, journaling and reminders; the welcome screen of the prototype.

outcome

Result

Designed around minutes, not hours.

The final prototype opens with a single prompt, “How are you feeling today?”, and personalises content around the response, recommending a short meditation, a playlist or a small action such as drinking water or calling a friend.

A weekly summary acknowledges small achievements, reinforcing positive habits without turning self-care into another task list.

Final design

Helping mothers thrive, one moment at a time.

Liv brings together a warm, approachable identity, a calm interface and features designed around the real time constraints of its users.

The project demonstrated how a focused set of brand decisions, a single logo and a five-colour palette, can define the character of an entire product when applied consistently across a team.
